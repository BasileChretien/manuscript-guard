# What is checked

Each gate of `manuscript-guard check`, by its number, and what it holds. The README sums
them up by what each is about ([What is checked](../README.md#what-is-checked)).

## G0 and BUILD

In place of a gate, a finding can carry one of two labels that are no gate of their own:
`G0`, for a file of the project that cannot be used as it stands, and `BUILD`, for what
the build would refuse or warn of.

Among what `BUILD` refuses at every stage is a document that hides text from its reader,
since a model reading it reads that text: text hidden by its own formatting or by a style,
too close in colour to what is behind it (a contrast ratio under 1.5), under 3 pt,
zero-width characters inside a word, and words that tell a model what to do where the
paper's reader does not look, such as its alternative text or its properties
(`document-hides-text`, `document-addresses-a-model`). The build checks the `.docx` it
writes and `submit` each one it packs; words to a model that the paper prints are a
warning, since a paper about them may quote them.

## What `check` ends with

`check` ends by grouping what it did by what it is about: computational reproducibility,
numerical provenance, reporting completeness, methodological validity, the data, the
literature, the review, and the language. For each it says which gates checked it and what
they found, what was recorded and by whom and when (a run of `verify`, a panel's reading, a
figure review, a value attested), and in words what no gate decides, such as whether the
design supports the conclusion. There is no score: a single number would be read as "the
paper is right", which no part of this says.

## Not every gate on the first day

You do not have to satisfy every gate on the first day. Each finding declares the stage at
which it starts to matter, from `design` to `submission`, and until then it is printed and
counted without failing the run: see [stages](stages.md). What each gate guarantees and
why it is built that way is in [principles](principles.md).
