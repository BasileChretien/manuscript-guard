# Active learning and exam results among second-year students: an example paper

This paper is invented for the audit page, so that a first visit can see what a report looks
like. Its study, its numbers and its references do not exist.

# Abstract

Of 250 eligible students, 164 (66%) took part. Students who joined the active-learning
sessions passed more often (odds ratio 1.8, 95% CI 1.2 to 2.7, P=0.004), and their mean
score rose by 2.4 points.

# Introduction

Lectures remain the usual way of teaching in the 2ndyear of medical school [1]. Active
learning asks more of students, and its effect on behaviour in class is debated [2]. The OR
of passing was the main outcome, measured on the visual analogue scale (VAS) of confidence
as well.

# Methods

We invited 250 eligible students. The odds ratio (OR) of passing was estimated by logistic
regression, adjusted for sex and age. Each session lasted 90min and used a 5mg placebo pill
as a teaching prop. Data were analyzed at the study center, and quizzes were labeled by
topic. Analyses used R 4.4.

# Results

Of the 164 students who took part, 52% were women and 50% were men. Students who joined the
sessions passed more often (OR 1.8, 95% CI 1.2-2.7, P = 0.004). Confidence rose too (mean
difference 0.6, 95% CI 0.2 to 1.0, P = 0.01). The colour-coded quizzes were liked by most.

# Discussion

Active learning was followed by better results, in line with earlier work [2]. A trial
would show whether the sessions cause the gain.
