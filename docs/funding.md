# Supporting manuscript-guard

manuscript-guard is a public project under the MIT licence, written and maintained by one
person. This page lists the ways to support it: three that cost money and three that do not.

## What costs money

- **A research grant that names the project.** If a grant application or a funded project
  would use manuscript-guard, or needs a part of it built, name it there. The way to start is
  to open an issue ([issues](https://github.com/BasileChretien/manuscript-guard/issues)) that
  says what the project needs.
- **An institutional collaboration.** A group that wants the checks adapted to its journals,
  its reporting guidelines or its data setup can say so in an issue in the same way. Any work
  that comes out of it is released in this repository under the same licence.
- **GitHub Sponsors.** The Sponsor button on the repository page leads to the maintainer's
  profile at [github.com/sponsors/BasileChretien](https://github.com/sponsors/BasileChretien).

## What costs none

- **Journal profiles.** A profile quotes a journal's instructions for authors in the form the
  checks read. One for a journal you submit to helps the next author who submits there. See
  [journal profiles](journal-profiles.md).
- **Reporting checklist recipes.** A recipe turns a published checklist into the profile the
  build checks. A recipe for a guideline that is missing is a contribution; the licence of the
  checklist decides whether the generated profile can be committed.
- **Bug reports with a failing case.** The most useful report is the smallest text that shows
  a gate flagging something that is fine, or letting a defect through. The issue templates
  under `.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/` ask for that.

## What this page does not say

It states nothing about how the project is funded now, and a contribution, in money or in work,
buys no promise of support, of a feature or of a date. The contact is the repository's issues.
